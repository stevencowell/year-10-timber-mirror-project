Theory Block 3

Measuring and Marking

Accurate marking is where quality starts. A clean cut cannot fix a careless mark.

Key Idea

Measure from a consistent edge and check before cutting.

Use a datum edge, try square and sharp pencil or marking knife. Mark waste sides clearly so you cut on the correct side of the line.

Why It Matters

The mirror frame depends on matching lengths and square corners. Small marking errors can become visible gaps during dry fitting.

Accurate Marking Routine

  1. Choose one straight datum edge.
  2. Measure from the same end or edge each time.
  3. Use a try square for lines across the timber.
  4. Mark the waste side.
  5. Check the measurement before cutting.

Workshop Rule

If the measurement is important, check it twice. If the timber is expensive, check it three times and breathe like a normal human before cutting.

Evidence Photo

Accuracy Is Visible

A good folio photo shows the check, not just the timber. The square, pencil line and clamping setup all help prove accuracy before the frame is glued.

From Rough Timber To Accurate Parts

Marking out only works properly when the timber has a reliable reference face and edge. The original program used the FEWTEL idea for preparing stock: face, edge, width, thickness, end and length. Even if machines are used for some stages, students should understand the sequence because it explains why accuracy comes before speed.

Stage What It Means Why It Matters
Face Prepare one flat face. Gives a reliable reference surface.
Edge Make one edge straight and square to the face. Creates the datum edge for measuring and marking.
Width / thickness Bring timber to the required size. Helps all frame parts match and sit flat.
End / length Square the end, then cut to final length. Controls gaps when the frame is dry-fitted.

Common Accuracy Faults

  • Measuring from different ends and getting mismatched frame rails.
  • Cutting directly through the line instead of allowing for the saw kerf.
  • Forgetting to mark the waste side and making the part too short.
  • Using a damaged edge as the datum and transferring the error into every cut.
  • Not checking diagonal measurements during dry fit.
