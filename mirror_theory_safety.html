Theory Block 1

Plan and Prepare

Before making the timber mirror, use the drawing to identify the requirements, plan the materials and choose a safe, accurate construction sequence.

Learning purpose

A project drawing controls the work. It is not just a picture to copy.

By the end of this section, you should be able to read the plan, identify the parts and materials needed, prepare a cutting list, and explain how planning improves safety and quality.

Use written dimensions

Use the measurements and notes printed on the supplied drawing. Do not measure a plan from a screen or printout because it may not be displayed at full scale.

Teacher check

Confirm cutting sizes, joints and machining details with your teacher before cutting timber. A small planning error is much easier to correct than a wrong cut.

1. Start with the project plan

The drawing tells you the overall frame size, mirror opening, rebate details, component shapes and construction notes. Read the whole drawing before you begin. The frame opening is deliberately smaller than the mirror so the timber supports it around the edges.

Workshop check

Find the written dimensions, joint details and material notes in the drawing. If a detail is unclear, stop and ask. Do not fill in missing information with a guess.

2. Identify the project requirements

A successful timber mirror needs to hold the mirror securely, stay flat and square, use accurate joints, have a smooth finish, and be safe to make, use and hang. These are the design criteria: the things you will check during construction and evaluation.

3. Prepare a materials and cutting plan

Before cutting, list the frame members, mirror, backing, trim or profile pieces, approved adhesive or fasteners, finish and hanging hardware. A cutting list records the component name, quantity and finished size.

  • Check that enough material is available before starting.
  • Place parts around knots, splits, bowing or other defects.
  • Prepare matching parts as pairs so they stay consistent.
  • Allow extra length only where a later dressing or machining step needs it.
  • Use the same datum or reference face when marking matching components.

4. Plan the work sequence

  1. Inspect the drawing and identify every component.
  2. Inspect timber for defects, grain direction and straightness.
  3. Prepare and confirm the cutting list.
  4. Select reference faces and edges, then mark from the same datum.
  5. Cut, dress and check parts before making joints.
  6. Dry-fit the frame and check it is square before gluing.
  7. Complete rebates, profiles, sanding, finishing and mirror fitting as directed.

The exact order can change with the tools and joints being used. For example, some rebate work is safer while timber is still in longer pieces. Follow the teacher's direction for each process.

Project outcome

Plan for a mirror that is accurate and safe

Use the drawing and your cutting plan to keep the frame square, support the mirror properly and make each workshop stage manageable before the first cut is made.

5. Plan safety before starting

Planning includes deciding how hazards will be controlled. Before practical work, complete the required safety instruction, wear the required PPE, secure loose clothing and hair, inspect timber for hazards, keep the work area clear, and obtain approval before machining or routing.

Never alter a dimension simply because a cut has gone wrong. Stop, check the drawing and discuss the safest correction with your teacher.
