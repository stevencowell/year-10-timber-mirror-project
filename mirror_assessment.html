Student Checklist

What Must Be Submitted

Finished Product

  • Completed timber mirror frame.
  • Accurate, square and secure frame construction.
  • Appropriate joint method completed safely.
  • Mirror/backing fitted under teacher direction.
  • Sanded and finished to a suitable standard.
  • Workshop cleaned and tools returned.

Portfolio Evidence

  • Design brief and target user.
  • Sketches or design notes.
  • Materials and cutting list.
  • Process photos with short captions.
  • Problems and solutions.
  • Peer quality check, feedback-used decision and workshop handover record.
  • Safety reflection.
  • Final evaluation.

Student Rubric

Achievement Levels

Use this before submission to judge the quality of your project and portfolio. The goal is not perfection; it is safe, accurate work with clear evidence.

High

Independent And Accurate

Work is safe, accurate and well finished. The portfolio clearly explains planning, evidence, problems, fixes and evaluation.

Sound

Mostly Controlled

Work is mostly safe and accurate with minor gaps or finish issues. The portfolio includes the main evidence and explains most choices.

Developing

Needs More Control

Work shows some correct process, but needs more accuracy, safer routines, clearer finish quality or stronger portfolio evidence.

Limited

Incomplete Or Unclear

Work is incomplete, unsafe without support, poorly evidenced, or does not yet show enough understanding of the mirror project.

Marking Guide

Mirror Project Criteria

This table shows how quality changes across the project. Aim for safe, controlled work and clear evidence, not last-minute guesswork.

Area High Sound Developing Limited
Safety Uses PPE, clamps, safe zones and tool routines independently. Usually works safely and responds to teacher reminders. Needs regular prompting to control hazards and slow down. Unsafe or incomplete routines stop progress without close support.
Accuracy Measures, marks, cuts and checks parts carefully with minimal gaps. Mostly accurate with small errors that are corrected or managed. Several errors affect fit, square or final appearance. Measurements or cuts are missing, unclear or prevent assembly.
Joinery And Assembly Joints fit neatly, hold securely and support a square frame. Joints mostly fit and hold, with minor gaps or alignment issues. Joints need adjustment, show gaps or rely heavily on teacher rescue. Joints are incomplete, loose, forced or not suitable for the frame.
Finish And Presentation Surface is smooth, edges are clean and finish is even. Finish is suitable with minor sanding or coating issues. Visible scratches, glue marks or uneven finish reduce quality. Finish is incomplete, rough, unsafe or poorly prepared.
Portfolio Evidence Clear design, cutting list, process photos, problem solving and evaluation. Main evidence is present with mostly useful captions and reflection. Evidence is thin, captions are vague or some sections are missing. Little evidence is submitted or it does not explain the project.
Peer Quality Check And Handover Records a specific criterion, observable evidence, feedback, a justified decision, result check and accountable handover. Records useful feedback and a response, with a clear handover contribution. Feedback, decision or handover record is vague, incomplete or weakly evidenced. No usable peer quality-check or handover evidence is provided.

Informal course practice

Knowledge Revision

This revision is classwork/practice. It is not an additional formal weighted Mirror assessment.

Know

  • Safety rules and PPE.
  • Timber terms and defects.
  • Marking and measuring tools.
  • Joint names and parts.

Explain

  • Why dry-fitting matters.
  • Why finishing needs surface preparation.
  • How to reduce waste.
  • How to evaluate a finished project.

Practise

  • Label a mortise and tenon joint.
  • Read a simple cutting list.
  • Choose safe controls for hazards.
  • Complete the quiz page.
